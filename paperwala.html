Paperwala

A 6 AM paper route. Draw the path, beat the clock, deliver the news.

Overview Core loop Mechanics Principles Levels Game jam Beyond the jam Art and language

Overview

Paperwala puts you on the saddle of a newspaper boy, the akhbaar wala who reaches every doorstep before the day begins. It is 6 AM and the clock runs to 8 AM. Drag your finger along the road to draw a route through every newspaper, release, and watch your cycle ride the route, tossing papers over the fence to each house.

The game turns something every Indian household knows, the morning paper arriving on time, into a short, readable puzzle: what is the best route? Each level adds one new real-world obstacle, such as cows, petrol and traffic. I made it for the Dainik Bhaskar Game Lab 2026.

Core loop

Mechanics

Drawing the route

The route must stay on the road and pass through every newspaper. Missing one shows “Connect all the newspapers first!” and lets the player redraw, and drawing off the road shows a warning. Failure is never silent.

The in-game clock runs from 6:00 to 8:00 AM while the cycle rides. Its digits turn green, then yellow, then red, and it throbs in the last half hour, so the player feels the urgency without reading numbers. Each newspaper is thrown towards the nearest house as the cycle passes, a small reward at every step.

Start, stop and cows

While riding, the player can press Stop and then Start. The clock only runs while the cycle moves, so stopping costs no time.

Cows come in two types. Ambush cows wait off the road and step onto it as the cycle approaches, so the player must stop before reaching one and wait until it walks off. Patrol cows walk back and forth across the road, pausing mid-road, so the player must time the crossing. Hitting a cow fails the level.

Skill it adds: reading the road ahead.

Petrol

In the town the cycle becomes a motorbike with a fuel gauge. A route that is too long runs dry. Petrol pumps on the road stop the bike to refuel for a few seconds, so route length and pump placement become the puzzle.

Skill it adds: planning and resource management.

Cars

Cars loop around fixed routes. They honk when close, brake for the bike in their lane and wait at crossings. A collision fails the level, so the player uses Stop to let a car pass. Cars always follow the same pattern, so a failed attempt can be learned from and beaten.

Skill it adds: pattern recognition and timing.

Design principles

One new idea per level

The first level that uses a mechanic switches off failure on time and shows a hand that demonstrates the action. The player learns by doing, not by reading.

Everything is deterministic

Patrol cows and cars wait for the ride to start and then repeat the same pattern every attempt, and ambush cows always react the same way. A failure feels like the player's mistake, never luck, and a solution can be shared: “the trick is to stop at the second corner”.

Show, don't tell

Meaning is carried by picture, colour and sound: green, yellow and red clock digits, a pulsing newspaper icon, a throbbing fuel gauge, a horn before a car arrives, and a moo before a cow steps out.

Forgiving touch input

The drag area extends a little beyond the road tile, so a thick finger on a small, low-end phone can still follow a lane. It works with both touch and mouse.

Fail fast, retry instantly

Each failure (late, cow, fuel, car) has its own picture-led screen with “Play again” and “Level select”, so the player always knows why they failed.

Culturally rooted, never stereotyped

A warm, sunlit illustrated look, familiar sounds and a bilingual interface. There is no violence, and even failures are cartoon-friendly.

Level design

The prototype has twelve hand-built levels in two worlds. Each new mechanic appears in a quiet teaching level, is repeated in a practice level, and is then combined with earlier mechanics in a test level. That Teach, Practice, Test cycle repeats three times (basic route, cows, then fuel and cars), so a player who stalls always has an easier level just behind them.

Tuned with a few numbers

Each level is tuned with a handful of designer-friendly numbers: clock seconds, ride speed, fuel range, starting fuel, refuel time, and car start delay and offset. Levels can be balanced quickly without code changes. They are built from Unity tilemaps with Road, Goals, Houses, Petrol and Animal layers, so a new level takes minutes to make: paint the road, drop in newspapers and houses, and place the hazards.

Designing for the game jam

The Dainik Bhaskar Game Lab 2026 asked for a game for a mobile news platform that millions of users would love to play every day, with mass appeal, daily engagement, simple mechanics and relevance to an Indian audience. Paperwala is a mobile prototype built in Unity, playable in English and Hindi.

BriefHow Paperwala answers it
Mass appeal and simplicity One gesture: drag a line, lift your finger. There are no text-heavy menus and no controls to memorise. A tutorial hand shows the first route, and each later mechanic is taught by playing the first level that uses it.
Indian audience relevance Village lanes and busy town roads, with everyday sights: a paper-boy on a cycle, cows on the road, a petrol pump and honking cars. The whole game, including every message and result screen, is playable in Hindi as well as English. The theme is a tribute to the newspaper itself.
Simple mechanics, hard to master Drawing a path is trivial. Drawing the efficient, safe path under time, fuel and traffic limits is the puzzle.
Fun and delight Papers spin through the air in an arc onto doorsteps, the cycle bell rings, cows moo, car horns blare, and a clock hand sweeps towards 8 AM. Every ride is a small show.
Daily engagement The 6 to 8 AM morning ritual matches the real habit of reading the news each morning.

Beyond the jam: daily play

The prototype delivers a complete 12-level campaign. Because levels are built from tile layers and a few tuning numbers, the same system is ready to grow into the daily-habit loop the brief asks for. These are the directions I planned, not features in the prototype.

Daily Route

One new map each morning, with the 6 to 8 AM clock tied to the real morning routine, in a short session of one or two minutes.

Content and events

New hazards such as rain, a school-bus rush, festival processions and a market day, plus seasonal maps, all added as new tile layers and obstacles.

Stars and streaks

Rewards for a faster or fuel-efficient route and for consecutive days played, with no ads, purchases or real-money rewards.

News integration

The newspaper theme fits naturally into a news app: a delivered paper could show that day's headline, linking play to reading.

Social play

Share route screenshots, or challenge a friend to deliver the same route faster.

Art, audio and language

Art

A bright, sunlit, illustrated 2D cartoon style, with separate village and city tile sets, houses, trees, bushes, cars, a cow and a cycle or motorbike with exhaust smoke. The interface uses wooden-sign buttons.

Audio

Separate background music for the title, the village and the city, and sound effects for the cycle bell, bike engine, cow, car horn, newspaper throw, warnings, and level complete and fail.

Localisation

A language choice on the title screen, English or Hindi, is saved and applied everywhere: instruction messages, warnings, buttons, level select and every result screen.
