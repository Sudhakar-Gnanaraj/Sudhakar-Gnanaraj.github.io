Orbital Defense

Command Earth's orbital defenses and defend humanity in this sci-fi tower defense game.

Overview

Orbital Defense is a sci-fi tower defense game I made in seven days for The Very Serious Juniper Dev Game Jam, where the theme was “Spin to Win”. An alien fleet is moving inward from Pluto towards Earth, and you command humanity's orbital defense network. Instead of placing towers on a grid, you drag weapon satellites around a planet's orbit so that their line of fire crosses the incoming ships.

Core loop

Gameplay and systems

Aiming by position

Satellites stay locked to their orbit and every one fires straight outward. Dragging a satellite around the orbit changes where its line of fire points, so positioning is the core skill: line up shots so that they cross an incoming ship's path.

Stasis Field

A draggable arc that slows everything inside it. Slowing a wave buys time to line up shots.

Scrap and upgrades

Destroyed enemies drop scrap. Right-click a weapon to buy upgrades: each weapon has two upgrade tracks of up to five levels, and every purchase doubles the cost.

Story and tutorial

Each level opens with a briefing: dialog with character portraits and typewriter text. Tooltip cards introduce each new enemy and weapon.

Campaign: five planets, 72 waves

In the jam version every level took place on Earth. After the jam I gave each level its own planet, with its own defense network, so the fresh start in each level has a reason. Each level also introduces a new enemy and the weapon that answers it.

Learning from player feedback

After the jam I read every comment on the game and shipped a July update that addressed the most common complaints. Players also had plenty of good things to say.

Comments from jam players, on the game's jam page. My jam entry and results ↗

Feedback 1

The Stasis Field was hard to grab

Problem

Six or more players could not reliably drag the Stasis Field. Clicking its edges or end handles did nothing, and only its centre responded.

“the hitbox on the slowing field is really weird”

Fix

I fixed the hit detection, so the field can now be grabbed from anywhere along its arc, including both end handles. I had always grabbed the middle while testing, so I never noticed.

Feedback 2

Upgrades reset every level

Problem

At least three players said it felt less rewarding to start upgrading from scratch in every level.

“a shame upgrades are reset in every level”

Fix

Rather than just carrying upgrades over, I treated this as a chance to expand the story. Every level now takes place on a different planet, from Pluto to Earth, each with its own defense network to upgrade independently. That keeps each level fresh and explains the fresh start.

Takeaway: A complaint about a rule can sometimes be answered by changing the fiction around the rule instead of the rule itself.

Feedback 3

The early levels were too punishing

Problem

Players found the first levels hard to get into, before they had learned the mechanics.

“the game could be a bit easier for the starting levels”

Fix

I rebalanced the early levels so that difficulty ramps up gradually. The late game keeps its challenge, but players now have time to learn the mechanics before things get intense.

Feedback 4

Aiming felt imprecise

Problem

Players couldn't see exactly where a weapon's shots would go, so repositioning satellites felt like guesswork.

“Positioning the weapons was not super easy at first”

Fix

Every weapon now draws a visual sightline, so players can see exactly where their shots will go.

Feedback 5

Level 1 undersold the game

Problem

The first levels showed only a few of the weapons, so the game looked simpler than it really was.

“Level 1 is misleading, makes the game seem way too simple”

Fix

All the weapons are now visible from the start but locked, with a hover hint showing when each one unlocks. New players see the full scope of the game right away and have something to look forward to.
