Orbital Defense 2

Overview Core gameplay Key systems Level design UI/UX

Overview

Orbital Defense is a 2D top-down tower-defense game. You defend Earth by placing satellites on orbit rings and shooting down waves of incoming enemies. Each satellite can swing along its orbit. You spend currency on building and boosting them.

This game is the sequel to Orbital Defense, a game I made in seven days for The Very Serious Juniper Dev Game Jam. I made this second game after getting ideas from the first one.

Core gameplay

Key systems

Orbit and placement

  • Each orbit ring has equally spaced, fixed build slots.
  • Each built satellite swings independently within its own arc.
  • That makes placement a real decision: slot position and swing range determine which approaching enemies a tower covers.

Economy loop

  • Kills drop . They are spent on building towers, buying upgrades and running override. Selling a tower refunds part of its cost.

Override (risk/reward)

  • Override fires 3x faster for a gun, and the laser beam becomes twice as thick.
  • It drains over time, so it is a deliberate burst-for-cash trade, usually saved for hard waves.

Enemy counterplay

  • Armor needs cannons, shields need lasers, and healers need to be prioritized.
  • Shield generators and protectors add layered defenses.

Strengths

  • A data-driven pipeline: designers can build a level from wave lists, ScriptableObject towers and upgrades, with no code changes.
  • Systems that interlock: armor, shield, healer, upgrade and override all create trade-offs.
  • Considered edge cases, such as support enemies never spawning alone and replays starting clean.

Level design

Design framework

I paced Orbital Defense's 12 levels with three measures:

  • Novelty: how much is new (mechanics, enemies, towers, locations).
  • Intensity: how much pressure the waves put on the player's defense.
  • Tension: how much is at stake, which comes mostly from Earth's HP carrying over between levels.

Intensity and tension are kept separate on purpose. A level can feel easy to fight and still be stressful to survive, because health carries forward.

The 4-level cycle

Level in cycleNoveltyIntensityTensionWhat the player feels
1: Introduction High Low Low Everything is new: mechanics, satellites, enemies. Time to learn without pressure.
2: Ramp-up Small spike Rising Rising A new enemy type. Waves start testing the player's build.
3: Peak pressure Small spike Highest Building The hardest waves in the cycle. Mistakes start costing health.
4: The squeeze Low Drops Highest The player now has a strong orbit, but carried-over damage leaves little health. Every leaked ship matters.

At level 4, intensity and tension pull apart. The player has the firepower to handle any threat, but earlier levels have worn health down, so the challenge becomes precision instead of survival.

The repeating structure

The cycle repeats three times: levels 1–4, 5–8 and 9–12. Each new cycle resets and escalates:

  • New location: the story moves to a new planet.
  • Health reset: tension drops back down, which gives a release after the level-4 squeeze.
  • New towers: players gain new tools at levels 5 and 9.
  • New core mechanic: armored enemies arrive at level 5 and shielded enemies at level 9. Each one forces players to rethink their tower mix.
  • Higher baseline difficulty: each cycle follows the same shape at a higher level, so players recognize the rhythm while the challenge keeps growing.

A new enemy also arrives in every level, so novelty never flatlines between the big resets.

Why it works

  • Rhythm prevents fatigue. Tension peaks are followed by release (the health resets), so the player isn't under constant pressure.
  • Novelty comes before difficulty. New mechanics arrive at low-intensity moments, when the player has room to learn.
  • Carried-over health creates meaningful stakes. Damage from early levels shapes how the late levels feel, so every level matters.
  • The structure is familiar but escalating. Players learn the shape of a cycle, which builds anticipation for what comes next.

UI/UX Design

1Hierarchy

  • Only two persistent HUD elements, Earth HP and , so the player's eye goes to the battlefield.
  • Enemy health bars sit directly above the enemy. The shield bar stacks above the HP bar, so the more important layer is read first.
  • popups appear at the kill location, which ties each reward to its cause.

2Progressive disclosure

  • Towers unlock gradually. Locked ones show a lock icon in the build menu.
  • The UI hides on levels without an economy, which keeps early levels simple.
  • Short tutorial videos introduce each mechanic or enemy type when it first matters.

3Consistency

  • One interaction pattern: click a slot to build, click a tower to select, right-click to upgrade.
  • Consistent feedback: every failed purchase triggers the same blinking warning and sound.
  • Unified art and audio: hand-drawn art, Roboto typography and 8-bit sound throughout.

4Contrast

  • Distinct colours separate enemy types and their explosions, so threats are readable at a glance.
  • Red variants signal cost or insufficient funds against the normal look.
  • Override mode changes the beam colour and adds lightning, so you can see which towers are overclocked.
  • Selected-tower buttons stand out against the space background.

5Accessibility

  • Large world-space click targets: icons spread in a ring, not packed tightly.
  • Overlap protection: selecting an icon never accidentally opens another ring's menu.
  • No forced pause: the build menu stays open while play continues.
  • Game speed options (1x / 1.5x / 2x): players can slow down or speed up to their comfort.
  • Redundant feedback: warnings combine visual (blinking) and audio cues, so no single sense is required.

Current gaps: no colourblind mode, rebindable controls or text-size options. Needs to be worked on in the future.

6Proximity

  • Controls sit on the object they affect. Override and Aim buttons appear on the tower, build icons around the slot, and health bars on the enemy.
  • The enemy's HP and shield bars stack together.
  • Dialog and character portrait fade as one unit.

7Alignment

  • Build icons spread evenly in a ring around the slot.
  • Orbit slots are equally spaced around each ring.
