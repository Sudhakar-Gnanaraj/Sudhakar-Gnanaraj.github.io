Kingslayer

A 2D stealth platformer: sneak through a castle and kill the king without being seen.

Overview Core loop Level design Level maps Mechanics Systems UI/UX Design

Overview

Kingslayer is a 2D side-scrolling stealth game: an assassin sneaks through a castle to kill the king without being seen. It was built to practise level design, teaching a stealth ruleset one mechanic at a time and then combining them.

Core loop

Level design

Two levels, two rule sets

Level 1 builds the core loop. Thrown apples make noise, and guards within 10 units walk to where one lands, so the player can pull them off their patrol route.

Level 2 turns every kill into a decision about where the body will lie, because a guard who sees it ends the attempt.

Ending: a cutscene where the player walks up to the king and kills him, then “The King Has Been Slain” and a return to the title screen.

Mechanics as level design tools

Each mechanic was built so that where things are placed decides how a section plays.

Sightlines

Each guard sees along two rays, at head height and at foot height, and walls and barrels block them. Crouching behind a barrel hides the player because the barrel blocks the low ray and the high ray passes overhead, so barrel placement is the cover system.

Patrols

Each guard has its own patrol distance, wait time at the turnaround points and starting direction, which sets the timing windows the player has to read.

Low passages

Zones where the player cannot stand up or jump, which force a crouched route.

Verticality

Ledge grabbing and a climbing rack give alternate routes above the guards.

Fail state and checkpoints

Being seen gives a 3-second “You Have Been seen” beat, then a respawn at the last checkpoint. Checkpoints are spread out so a failure costs only a short section.

Readability

“?” and “!” icons over guards, and sightline visuals in calm, suspicious and alerted colors. When a guard finds a body, the camera pans to show where the player was caught.

Gameplay and systems

Gameplay features

  • Stealth movement: walk, jump, crouch, crouch-walk, ledge grab and climb-over
  • Throwable apples from crates, with a dotted arc that previews the throw
  • Noise distraction: guards walk to where the apple lands, wait, then return to their post
  • Knife assassinations, with a second fail state when a guard finds a body
  • Checkpoint respawns after being caught
  • Cutscenes for drawing the knife at the start of Level 2 and the final kill on the king

UI/UX Design

UX and accessibility

  • No pause-and-read tutorial screens: prompts sit in the level where the player needs them
  • Full controller support, with prompts that change to match the input device
  • Clear fail feedback (“You Have Been seen”, “Dead Body has been found”) and a quick respawn
  • Title screen → game → ending flow with fade transitions
